What CarbonKeel provides, and what it computes

A plain-English reference with the working shown: what goes in, what is calculated, how, and how far to trust each result.

6 October 2026 · worked examples use EMSA’s 2025 report, version 6

1The short version

CarbonKeel turns a ship’s publicly reported emissions into the money and ratings that follow from them, using the actual rules. Every figure comes from ordinary calculation code built from the regulations. No AI produces a number.

  • Input: a ship name or IMO number. Optionally its size (deadweight), or the operator’s own fuel and distance data.
  • Output: the carbon bill under the EU and UK emissions trading schemes and FuelEU Maritime, a CII efficiency grade (A to E), an EEXI design check, a scenario for the IMO Net-Zero Framework, the effect of two standard levers, and a fleet view with deadlines.
  • Honest about its basis: results rebuilt from public data are labelled ESTIMATE. Where the operator supplies its own data, the result is labelled OWNER DATA.
Read this first: which year is the bill for? The headline figure is the obligation for the latest reported year in the register (2025), under that year’s rules. For 2025 only 70% of emissions is surrendered; from 2026 it is 100%. The app therefore shows both: the 2025 bill, and what the same emissions would cost under the 2026 rules (see 4.2). Sections 6 and 7 list every basis and limit.

4What is computed, step by step

Each part below states the purpose, the method (with the maths written out), a worked example, and the limits. The example ship throughout is PETER OLDENDORFF (IMO 9464663, a bulk carrier), using EMSA’s 2025 report, version 6. It is a real, public ship, used only to show the working; the figures are estimates.

4.1 Rebuilding the ship’s year from the public file

Purpose

The file gives totals, not voyages. To apply rules that treat voyages differently (full, half, or not at all), we split the year into legs.

Method

  • One leg per scope: inside the EU, to or from the EU (departed plus arrived), at berth, and outside scope (whatever remains of the total).
  • Fuel in each leg = that leg’s CO₂ divided by the carbon factor of the assumed fuel.
  • Distance = total CO₂ (in kg) divided by the reported CO₂ per nautical mile, shared between legs in proportion to their CO₂. Berth has no distance.
distance = total CO2 (kg)reported CO2 per nautical mile    fuel = CO2 of the leg (t)carbon factor (t CO2 per t fuel)

4.2 EU Emissions Trading System (ETS)

Purpose

Shipping companies buy and hand in EU allowances for emissions in scope. This is usually the largest single number.

Method

allowances = Σlegs (CO2e of the leg × scope share) × phase-in
Scope share: voyages inside the EU 100%, to or from the EU 50%, at berth 100%, outside scope 0%. Phase-in of what is surrendered: 40% for 2024, 70% for 2025, 100% from 2026.
cost = allowances × allowance price (€70 assumed)

From 2026, methane and nitrous oxide count as well. CO₂e = CO₂ + CH₄ × 28 + N₂O × 265 (the global-warming potentials used by the EU ETS). For liquid fuels CH₄ is 0.00005 and N₂O is 0.00018 tonnes per tonne of fuel; for LNG the methane slip depends on the engine type.

Step (example)WorkingResult
ETS-scope CO₂603 + 0.5 × (5,347 + 7,494) + 3867,409 t (the verifier reported 7,409)
Allowances due, 2025 rules7,409 × 70%5,186
Cost5,186 × €70€363,039
Same emissions, 2026 rulesCO₂e 7,526 t (incl. CH₄, N₂O) × 100% × €70€526,805
Be clear about what this is. For a ship in the public file, the ETS figure is essentially the verifier’s own scope CO₂, weighted by the scope rules, then multiplied by the phase-in and the assumed price. Our value is the rule application, the 2026 view and the levers, not an independent measurement. The price is an assumption; the app also shows the result at €60 and €100.

4.3 FuelEU Maritime

Purpose

FuelEU limits the greenhouse-gas intensity of the energy a ship uses (not its tonnes). A ship above the limit has a deficit and a penalty; below it, a surplus.

Method

intensity = Σ (fuel t × energy per t × well-to-wake gCO2e/MJ)Σ (fuel t × energy per t)
Counted over the energy in scope: voyages inside the EU 100%, to or from the EU 50%, berth 100%.
balance = (target − intensity) × energy in scope    penalty = |balance|intensity × 41,000 × €2,400
Penalty applies only to a deficit (balance below zero). 41,000 MJ is the energy of one tonne of very-low-sulphur fuel oil.

The target is the 2020 baseline of 91.16 gCO₂e/MJ reduced by 2% from 2025, 6% from 2030, 14.5% from 2035, 31% from 2040, 62% from 2045 and 80% from 2050. The fuel values are the defaults in Annex II of the regulation:

An operator’s own fuel data corrects this.

4.4 UK Emissions Trading Scheme

Purpose

The UK scheme covers shipping from 1 July 2026. It applies to domestic UK voyages only: UK port to UK port at 100%, voyages between Great Britain and Northern Ireland at 50%, and time at berth in UK ports at 100%. It counts CO₂, methane and nitrous oxide from the start and has no phase-in.

Method

cost (£) = Σlegs (CO2e × UK share) × UK allowance price (£40 assumed)
Shown in euros in the combined figure at €1.15 per £. The first scheme period is the second half of 2026.
Limit. The EU register does not show UK domestic voyages, so from public data this line is normally zero (“No UK domestic voyages found”). It becomes meaningful only with the operator’s own voyage data.

4.5 IMO Net-Zero Framework (scenario only)

Purpose

A proposed global scheme, approved in principle in April 2025, with the adoption vote expected in October 2026. Because it is not adopted, it is shown only as a scenario, and the parameters are provisional.

Method

GFI = Σ (fuel t × energy per t × well-to-wake)Σ (fuel t × energy per t)
Counted over all fuel burned on all voyages, not only the EU part. Reference: 93.3 gCO2e/MJ.

Two targets fall each year from the 93.3 reference (2028: Base −4%, Direct −17%; 2030: −8% and −21%; 2035: −30% and −43%). Above the Base target the ship buys Tier 2 remedial units ($380 per tonne) and also Tier 1 for the band between the two targets ($100); between the targets only Tier 1; below Direct it earns surplus units.

4.6 CII: the A to E efficiency grade

Purpose

The Carbon Intensity Indicator grades how much CO₂ a ship emits to move its capacity over a distance, against a limit that tightens every year to 2030. Three years at D, or one at E, requires a corrective plan.

Method (IMO guidelines G1 to G4)

attained = Σ (fuel t × carbon factor) × 106capacity × distance (nm)    reference = a × capacity−c
required = reference × (1 − Z100 )    ratio = attainedrequired
The rating is the first band whose boundary the ratio does not exceed. Boundaries = required × d1, d2, d3, d4.
  • Capacity is deadweight for most ship types and gross tonnage for ro-ro, ro-pax, cruise and vehicle carriers.
  • Z (the reduction against the 2019 reference): 5% (2023), 7%, 9%, 11% (2026), 13.625% (2027), 16.25%, 18.875%, 21.5% (2030).
  • a, c and d1 to d4 depend on the ship type. For bulk carriers: a = 4,745, c = 0.622; boundaries at 0.86, 0.94, 1.06 and 1.18 of the required value.

The emissions and distance cover only voyages touching EU ports, so the grade describes that part of the operation, while the real CII covers the whole year. Treat it as a screening grade, not a certified rating.

The operator’s calculator (owner data)

The operator enters fuel by type and distance sailed, or estimates from speed. The speed route uses the cube law: main-engine fuel per day = reference consumption × (actual speed ÷ reference speed)3; distance = speed × 24 × days at sea; auxiliary fuel is added. Voyage adjustments (the G5 corrections) are applied only if the operator supplies documented values; we never invent one. The result adds:

  • The path to 2030: the same performance against each year’s tighter limit.
  • The cut needed to reach each band: (1 − boundary ÷ attained) × 100%.

4.7 EEXI: the design check

Purpose

The Energy Efficiency Existing Ship Index is a one-off design check that most ships passed in 2023. It matters again for newbuildings, conversions and changes to engine power limits, so it is free and not the headline.

Method

required EEXI = (1 − X100 ) × a × capacity−c
X is the reduction factor for the ship type and size (MARPOL Annex VI, Regulation 25, Table 3). The verdict is: attained at or below required passes.
  • For a ship in the register: compare the value it reported against the required value. If it reported an EEDI rather than an EEXI, the verdict is labelled INDICATIVE.
  • Capacity basis: deadweight for most types; gross tonnage for passenger and cruise ships; for container ships, 70% of deadweight for the attained value but the full deadweight for the required value (a rule that, applied the other way, would let a failing ship look compliant).
  • For a custom ship, give an attained EEXI, or let us estimate it from engine power and speed:
attained ≈ (PME × CF × SFOCME) + (PAE × CF × SFOCAE)capacity × speed
Main engine power = 75% of MCR; auxiliary power = 2.5% of MCR + 250 kW (5% below 10,000 kW); specific fuel consumption 175 and 215 g/kWh (for heavy fuel oil).
Example (custom bulk carrier)WorkingResult
Inputs93,000 dwt; MCR 12,000 kW; service speed 14.2 kn; heavy fuel oil

The estimate leaves out power take-off, shaft generators, ice class, innovative technologies and the weather factor, so the certified Technical File value will differ. That is why the verdict says INDICATIVE.

4.8 The levers: biofuel and slower speed

Purpose

To show, in euros, what two standard operational changes would do to the carbon bill. The one-click “Value identified” uses a standard package: 20% of fossil energy replaced by 100% biofuel (B100) on EU voyages, and a 10% lower speed.

Method

  • Slower speed (distance unchanged): fuel for the same distance scales with the square of speed, because engine power rises with the cube of speed while the passage takes proportionally longer. So tonnes × (1 + speed change)2; a 10% cut gives × 0.81.
  • Biofuel: the chosen share of each fossil fuel’s energy is replaced by B100, with the tonnage corrected for its lower energy content (about 10% more tonnes). B100 is treated as zero-rated under the EU ETS and CII (this assumes it is certified), and has a well-to-wake intensity of 16.4 under FuelEU.

What this figure is not. It is a reduction in carbon cost. It does not subtract the cost of buying the biofuel, which is the main cost of the lever, nor the effect of slower sailing on voyage time and chartering, nor certification. It assumes the biofuel is certified. The app says “before the cost of the biofuel itself” for this reason. Read it as the size of the prize, not a net saving.

4.9 Fleet analysis and pooling

Purpose

A manager wants the whole fleet at once. Fleet totals are the sums of the per-ship figures. The pooling step looks at FuelEU, where ships may pool their balances: if the pool’s total balance is zero or better, every member is treated as compliant.

Method

  • All ships with a surplus contribute their surplus tonnes to the pool’s capacity.
  • Choosing which deficit ships to admit is a classic knapsack problem: each costs |deficit| tonnes of capacity and would otherwise pay its own penalty. We choose the set that avoids the most penalty without exceeding the capacity. It is solved exactly at 1-tonne steps for fleets up to a large size, with a fast approximation above that.
  • The registration deadline is 30 April of the following year.
Limit. Because the public estimate assumes conventional fuel (4.3), a fleet of ordinary ships usually has no surplus, and the app then says there is no spare pooling capacity inside the fleet. We do not match counterparties between companies. The “draft pooling agreement” is a template, not legal advice.

7Known limits and open items

  • Data version. The figures use the 2025 report, version 6. EMSA has published version 60. A refresh tool is built and waits for approval; numbers will change.
  • Partial-year reports. EMSA’s file has a separate sheet of partial reports that we do not yet read. We will test whether it explains part of the 21.8% mismatch.
  • Fuel type. Inferred, not published. This drives FuelEU, NZF and the methane and nitrous-oxide line from 2026.
  • Capacity. Deadweight and gross tonnage are not in the public file; CII needs them.
  • Allowance prices are fixed assumptions. We do not use live market prices.
  • Biofuel in the levers is assumed certified and zero-rated; real certification and pathway values vary.
  • Ship types. Twelve types are supported. Others (for example offshore and generic passenger ships) are not scored; a custom EEXI check is offered instead.
  • Methane slip for LNG depends on the engine type, which the public file does not give; the default is the medium-speed dual-fuel value.
  • NZF parameters follow the April 2025 approval and must be checked against the adopted text.
